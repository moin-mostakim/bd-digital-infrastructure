Story · Scenario I

Karim’s morning in Tangail

5:40 AM · Bhatgram village · 2G only on a button phone

Karim Mia loads ৫০ মন ধান onto a van for Rahim Uddin, a rice trader in Mirpur. They agreed on ৪২ টাকা per kg yesterday—verbally, with no written record.

Rahim calls: “Send me proof before the van leaves. My accountant needs Bangla details.” Karim has no smartphone, no bKash app on device, and spotty literacy in English SMS templates.

What happens today (typical)

  • Handwritten note on paper—lost, disputed, or unreadable after rain
  • Voice call only—no itemized price, no receipt ID, no tax trail
  • Smartphone apps exist (bKash merchant) but exclude this handset
  • Cooperative ledgers updated days later at union office

Karim’s world runs on voice + SMS—not apps.

Story · Scenario II

Anwar’s crate at Chittagong port

11 PM · Port gate · CCTV exists, systems do not talk

Anwar Hossain, an garments accessories trader, clears a crate marked AH-4471. By morning it is rerouted to the wrong bonded warehouse. His paper challan matches—digitally, nothing links the crate to his face, his NID, or the truck that moved it.

Police ask for proof of custody. Port CCTV has footage; NID database has his photo; customs has a scan log—but no one query connects them in time across districts.

What happens today (typical)

  • Biometric NID photos—not wired to live port face search
  • CCTV vendors differ; footage stays local
  • Challan partly digital, partly paper; gaps between weighbridge & warehouse
  • Cross-ministry data sharing is manual, slow, or blocked
Story · China (contrast)

Li Wei’s evening at Guangzhou Baiyun Airport

9:15 PM · Terminal 2 · Return from Frankfurt

Li Wei, a textile machinery buyer, rolls his suitcase toward the domestic transfer gate. He has no boarding pass printout—only his passport and a WeChat health code from years ago. He is not thinking about technology.

A ceiling camera captures his face. In under four seconds, the border system matches him to his national travel ID: last entry (March), hotel registration in Shenzhen, high-speed rail ticket to Guangzhou South, and the duty-free purchase he forgot he made.

When his suitcase fails to appear on the carousel, staff at the baggage desk type his passport number—or simply confirm the face match on their screen. The bag’s tag, linked to the same identity graph, shows it was misloaded to Hangzhou. A redirect is ordered before Li Wei reaches the complaint counter.

What the story shows (systems, not magic)

  • Face 1:N at the gate, tied to lawful identity records
  • One graph for travel, lodging, tickets, and baggage events
  • Staff tools that answer “who is this and what moved with them?” quickly
  • Policy + plumbing built over years—not a single app download

Bridge to Bangladesh

Anwar at Chittagong has cameras and NID too—but without a fused graph and query broker, his crate story stops at the first silo. TrailGuard is a proposal to build that pipe lawfully, not copy every policy choice.

Scenario I · Today

Rural commerce on 2G—current reality

  • Devices: Tens of millions of feature phones; voice + SMS + USSD; GPRS often off or unused
  • Language: Business done in Bangla; official SMS often English or numeric codes
  • Payments: bKash/Nagad widespread but usually need smartphone or agent—not field invoice from Karim’s phone
  • Records: Cooperatives, NGOs, and buyers want digital audit trails farmers cannot produce at gate

What 2G button phones already support

  • USSD menus (*123# style)—session-based, no internet
  • SMS—outbound invoice to any mobile
  • IVR—Bangla voice menus (operator-hosted)
  • STK on some SIMs (limited)—not assumed for Karim

Gap: No national “Krishi receipt” pipe tying these channels to a trusted ledger.

Scenario I · Problem

Why Karim still cannot send a proper invoice

  1. App bias: Product teams design for Android; Karim is invisible in requirements
  2. Fragmented USSD: Each pilot uses a different short code; no open SDK for cooperatives
  3. Bangla gap: Amounts, units (মন, কেজি), and legal wording not standardized in SMS
  4. Trust gap: Buyer cannot verify SMS came from Karim vs spoofed sender
  5. Connectivity: USSD timeout when tower load spikes at harvest season

Karim’s pain in one line:

“Van is leaving. I have 30 seconds and twelve buttons—not an app store.”

Scenario I · Build

What developers & infrastructure provide

  • USSD gateway — map telco XML/session → your REST API
  • Bangla layer — Unicode SMS, Bengali numerals, unit dictionary
  • Signing service — HMAC receipt ID; anti-spoof sender registry with operator
  • Co-op dashboard — web app for exports (not on farmer phone)

Infrastructure checklist

  • Commercial SMS route + dedicated short code
  • BDIX-hosted API (latency & data residency)
  • Optional union Raspberry Pi / mini-PC store-and-forward
  • IVR fallback for low-literacy users (Bangla TTS)

Scenario II · Problem

Why Anwar’s crate vanishes in the data

  2. Face match not operational at gate—not because algorithms absent, but pipes & policy aren’t

Scenario II · Proposed solution

TrailGuard—lawful national logistics graph

Concept

Cameras & scanners emit signed events → face embed service (scoped) → query broker returns ranked trails for a case ID—not open surveillance.
  • Detect → embed → match at edge; raw video short retention
  • Entity graph: person_id ↔ crate ↔ vehicle ↔ location
  • Query broker: customs/police submit warrant/case token
  • Human review for matches below confidence threshold
